Why do I break out before my period?

6 min read · Aperia Journal

If your skin flares in the days before your period — a little more oil, a few spots in the same familiar places — you're noticing one of the most common and most predictable patterns in skin. It isn't a coincidence, and it isn't something you did wrong that week. It's your hormones moving through their cycle, and your skin moving with them.

What's actually happening

Across the month, the balance of your hormones shifts in a wave. In the second half of the cycle — the stretch after ovulation and right up to your period — that balance tends to tip in a way that nudges the skin: pores can clog a little more easily, oil can pick up, and the skin can feel more reactive. So the week before your period becomes the week your skin is most likely to flare. Same window, month after month.

The premenstrual breakout isn't random. It's the most scheduled thing your skin does.

Why it's usually the same spots

People often notice their premenstrual breakouts cluster in the same zones — very commonly the chin and jaw, sometimes the cheeks. That's because those areas tend to respond most to the hormonal shift. Once you know your own map, a breakout there in that week stops being a mystery and becomes something you can almost predict.

What actually helps

The most useful thing isn't a new product bought in a panic the day a spot appears — it's seeing the timing. When you know a flare tends to arrive in a specific week, three things change. You stop blaming yourself. You stop reacting to every spot as an emergency. And you start giving your skin steady, consistent care through the whole month instead of overhauling your routine the moment things look rough.

Beyond the cycle itself, the other big inputs still matter in that sensitive week: how you're sleeping, how much stress you're carrying, and — for some people — certain foods. A hard, low-sleep, high-stress week layered on top of the premenstrual window is often when skin looks its worst. Seeing those inputs together is what turns "why is this happening again?" into "oh — of course."

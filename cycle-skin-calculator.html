How your cycle can show up on your skin

Across a typical cycle, estrogen and progesterone rise and fall in a fairly regular wave. Skin responds to that wave — which is why many people notice the same changes at the same point every month. The pattern is different for everyone, but it often looks something like this:

PhaseTypical days*What skin often does
Period1–5Can feel drier, duller or more sensitive; last week's spots often start to calm.
Follicular6–12Rising estrogen — many people see calmer, more even, better-hydrated skin.
Ovulation13–15Often the "glow" days; oil may start to increase towards the end.
Early luteal16–21Progesterone rises; more oil and congestion are common.
Watch week22–28The days when breakouts most often appear — commonly chin and jawline.

*For a 28-day cycle. The calculator adjusts these to your own cycle length.

Questions

Why does my skin break out before my period?

In the days before a period, the balance of hormones shifts and many people notice more oil and breakouts, often around the chin and jawline. Not everyone gets them, and the timing differs from person to person — which is why tracking your own pattern is useful.

Which cycle phase is my skin usually at its best?

Many people find their skin looks clearest and most even in the days after their period and around ovulation, when estrogen is higher. Your own pattern may be different.

How accurate is this calculator?

It gives an estimate based on the cycle length you enter, assuming ovulation about 14 days before your next period. Real cycles vary month to month, so treat the dates as a guide, not a prediction. It is not suitable for contraception or fertility planning.

Does this work if I use hormonal contraception?

Most hormonal contraceptives change or pause the natural cycle phases, so the phase estimates here may not apply. Tracking your skin over time can still show you your own patterns.

Is my data stored?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser. Nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere.
